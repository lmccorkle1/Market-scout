Market Scout methodology

Market Scout is a research screen for roughly 3–24 month opportunities in companies experiencing significant price declines. It is not a recommendation, valuation model, or substitute for reviewing filings and current company information.

How the shortlist is built

  1. The scanner downloads one year of adjusted daily closing prices for the S&P 500 and the disclosed additional growth list.
  2. Every company receives a price-opportunity score based on its distance below the one-year high and 50-day average.
  3. The 40 strongest price-screen candidates are enriched with available company fundamentals.
  4. A company needs at least seven of the ten scoring inputs to qualify. Missing values receive no points and are displayed as unavailable.
  5. The five highest eligible quantitative scores become research candidates. Ties are ordered by ticker.

Manual catalyst reserve: 10 points

Market Scout does not automatically award the remaining 10 catalyst points. Recovery catalysts depend on current filings, earnings, management guidance, competitive conditions, and news. The research workspace lets the user record a catalyst and thesis without presenting those judgments as verified scanner output.

Personal criteria

The My Criteria table is separate from the published Top Five. It filters only the fundamentally enriched candidate pool and does not alter the scanner's baseline ranking. Market cap comes from the company-data source. Average dollar volume is the mean of adjusted closing price multiplied by reported volume over the latest 20 trading sessions. If an active rule requires a missing metric, that company does not pass the rule.

The Quality Selloff preset uses: market cap of at least $2 billion; average dollar volume of at least $10 million; non-negative revenue and earnings growth; debt/equity no greater than 150; trailing P/E no greater than 40; non-negative free-cash-flow yield; and a discount of at least 15% from the one-year high. These broad thresholds are a starting point, not a recommendation.

Quarterly trends and value-trap warnings

For enriched candidates, Market Scout compares the latest reported quarter with the corresponding quarter one year earlier. It displays revenue growth, operating margin and margin change, latest free cash flow, debt change, and share-count change. The comparison requires actual quarterly fields; missing values remain unavailable.

Automatic warnings currently identify: revenue declines greater than 5%; operating-margin contraction greater than three percentage points; a latest-quarter net loss; negative latest-quarter free cash flow; debt growth greater than 20%; and share-count growth greater than 5%. These warnings do not alter the quantitative ranking yet. They are research prompts, not proof that a company is or is not a value trap, and should be checked against SEC filings.

SEC XBRL cross-check

For each Top Five company, Market Scout attempts an independent comparison with the SEC's standardized Company Facts data. It uses reported 10-Q or 10-K facts whose periods are 70–110 days and end on the same quarterly dates used by the scanner. Revenue growth and operating-margin change are considered a match when each available comparison is within two percentage points.

Confirmed means both comparisons matched; partial means one available comparison matched; mismatch means at least one comparison differed; and unavailable means the SEC request or comparable standardized facts were unavailable. This cross-check does not change the ranking. An unavailable result is never treated as confirmation.

Price stabilization and relative strength

Every screened stock receives informational price-action signals calculated from adjusted daily closes. The scanner shows 5-, 20-, and 63-trading-day returns, position relative to the 20-day average, and distance above the 20-day low. “Stabilizing” requires a non-negative 5-day return, a close at or above the 20-day average, and a close at least 3% above the 20-day low. “Improving” meets two of those three tests. “Still falling” means the 5-day return is −3% or worse while price remains below its 20-day average; other combinations are “mixed.”

The 63-day sector-relative figure subtracts the median 63-day return of all successfully scanned companies in the same GICS sector. It is expressed in percentage points and includes the peer count. Unknown classifications are compared only with other unknown classifications. These signals do not alter the Top Five score yet; they are timing context, not predictions.

Sources and timing

  • Constituents, GICS classifications, and SEC CIK identifiers: Wikipedia's S&P 500 constituent table.
  • Prices, company fundamentals, company websites, and earnings dates: Yahoo Finance data accessed through the open-source yfinance library. Fields may be delayed, revised, missing, or subject to Yahoo's terms.
  • Filing links: the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission's public EDGAR company pages, using the published CIK when available.
  • Top Five cross-check: the SEC's public Company Facts API. Standardized facts can be missing or use company-specific tagging, and SEC access can be temporarily unavailable.
  • Scheduled scans: weekdays after the regular U.S. market close. GitHub Actions schedules may start late.

Important limitations: Scores use broad thresholds and are not adjusted for every industry's accounting conventions. Negative earnings can make valuation fields unavailable. Earnings dates can change. Historical performance, a low valuation multiple, or a large selloff does not guarantee recovery. Always verify the displayed information against current SEC filings and company disclosures.
